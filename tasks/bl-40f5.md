## The finding

`bl reopen` (bl-3628, landed `d652c76a`) is a redundant path. Every part of it
already existed, and the composition works today:

    bl show <closed-id> --json | bl import

| reopen did | what already does it |
|---|---|
| reconstruct the dead ball | `bl show <id> --json` — the bedrock read (§9) |
| write it back live | `bl import` (§9/§16) |
| refuse a live id | import's collision refusal, verbatim: *"id(s) already held: bl-x — refusing the whole stream, nothing imported (retire the holder or strip the record, then re-run)"* |
| refuse an id naming nothing | `bl show bl-0000` → *"no such ball: bl-0000"* |
| `--clean` (drop `claimant`) | `bl unclaim <id>` |

## Why this was always the answer

Reopening does not undo anything. The close commit stays, the deletion stays,
history is untouched. All that happens is a ball comes into being carrying an
id and content that already existed — which is exactly §9's definition of
`import`: it "reproduces already-identified balls," ingesting "a REPRODUCTION of
an identity that is authoritative at its source (a federation peer, a backup,
the §16 legacy store), **not a transition**." A retired ball in this store's own
history is another such source. §9 also states the bedrock record is TOTAL —
frontmatter AND body — *because* "`bl import` writes it back verbatim (bl-e614,
§16): what the bedrock reads out must be the whole ball." The round trip is the
feature. Reopen re-implemented one leg of it.

Reopen was also the WEAKER spelling in two ways. It restamped `updated` on the
premise that a restore is a transition — it is not, and import's verbatim
reproduction is the honest record. And it made the store's own history a special
kind of source, when it is an ordinary one.

## Scope

Revert `d652c76a` whole: the `Reopen` verb + base change, `--clean`, the guard,
`skill/reopen.md`, and their tests.

Keep the one thing that was genuinely missing — **discoverability**. The
composition is invisible to someone holding a closed id. That is a
documentation gap, and the fix is a doc line, not a verb:

- `skill/import.md` — name the round trip as a first-class use: reopening a
  closed ball is `bl show <id> --json | bl import`, and `bl unclaim` after if the
  restored `claimant` is stale.
- `skill/close.md` — a closed ball is recoverable, and here is how.
- `docs/architecture.md` §9 — replace the reopen entry with a sentence under
  `import` recording that the store's own history is a valid source and that
  this is why no `reopen` verb exists (the ban, so it is not re-proposed).
- `SKILL.md` — keep the "absence is older content" invariant sharpening, point
  it at the composition instead of the verb.

## The principle this cost

A verb is the last thing to reach for. The read side already reconstructed dead
balls and the write side already ingested verbatim records; "core can read the
dead set but not write it back" was a false asymmetry — the write existed, one
pipe away. Attack the primitives before building within them.
