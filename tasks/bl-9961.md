RECURRING ergonomic papercut (hit again on bl-98ba's delivery 2026-07-07). §5 says 'the subject is ALWAYS the ball title (there is no override); the optional -m text is the free body.' But the DELIVERY squash disagrees: delivery_message::compose (bl-b9a6) treats -m (override_msg) as a FULL subject+body OVERRIDE, highest precedence, and with_marker() inserts the [bl-id] tag after the first PHYSICAL line of it. So 'bl close -m "<multi-line rich body>"' — written intending the BODY, expecting the ball title as subject — instead makes the whole first PARAGRAPH the delivery subject, with [bl-id] wedged after wherever the first newline fell. You only get a clean commit if the -m's first line is a deliberately-short subject followed by a blank line.

WHY IT RECURS: the project rule is 'write rich commit bodies' (release-plz renders them into the CHANGELOG), so people naturally pass a rich multi-line -m to close — and every time it silently mangles the main-branch subject. The fix I used was to amend the commit in a throwaway 'git worktree add <p> main' — a papercut, not a solution.

THE INCONSISTENCY IS INTERNAL: the STORE seal already treats -m as body-only (subject = ball title, §5) via Message::render. Only the DELIVERY squash makes -m a full override. So one op writes -m two different ways.

DIRECTIONS (attack, don't settle):
(a) SUBTRACTION — make -m on close BODY-ONLY everywhere: delivery subject = ball title + [bl-id] ALWAYS (matching §5 and the store seal). Reconcile bl-b9a6 (rich work-branch context on the squash) by making the work-branch messages the squash BODY under the title subject, not a subject replacement. Cleanest; -m and work-context both become body, title is always the subject.
(b) keep the override but make with_marker robust: if the message has no blank-line-terminated subject, synthesize '<ball title> [id]\n\n<message as body>' instead of splitting on the first physical newline.
(c) document loudly (weakest — it keeps recurring).
Recommend (a): it removes a mode, restores the §5 invariant, and kills the papercut. Keep open for the maintainer's read.
